Maryam in Islam

Maryam bint ‘Imran (peace be upon her) — mother of the prophet ‘Isa (peace be upon him) — is honoured in the Qur’an in her own right, as a woman of devotion Allah chose and purified. Our masjid carries her name. Here is her story as the Qur’an tells it.

Photo: Mubashir Ahmet / Unsplash

From the Qur’an

Named, and named again

Maryam is the only woman mentioned by name in the entire Qur’an, and she is named again and again throughout it.

The nineteenth surah of the Qur’an is called Maryam, after her, and her story is also told at length in Surah Al-‘Imran — the surah named for her family.

وَٱذْكُرْ فِى ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ مَرْيَمَ

“And mention Maryam in the Book…”

Qur’an 19:16

Her story

As the Qur’an tells it

  1. Surah Al-Imran

    Her birth and dedication

    The Qur’an (3:35–37) tells of her mother, the wife of ‘Imran, who vowed to dedicate the child in her womb to the worship of Allah, expecting a boy. When a girl was born, she named her Maryam and prayed for her protection, and Allah accepted her with a gracious acceptance.

    Maryam grew up under the guardianship of the prophet Zakariyya (peace be upon him), who would find her prayer chamber provided with sustenance out of season. When he asked her where it came from, she answered: “It is from Allah. Indeed Allah provides for whom He wills without account.”

  2. Surah Maryam

    The glad tidings and the miraculous birth

    The Qur’an narrates the angel’s visit to Maryam (19:16–21) with the glad tidings of a pure son, her astonishment — no man had ever touched her — and the divine response: for Allah, that is easy. He creates what He wills; when He decrees a matter, He only says to it “Be,” and it is.

    She then withdrew to a remote place. The Qur’an’s account is intimate and human: she cries out in the pain of labour beneath a palm tree, and is comforted and provided for — a stream at her feet, ripe dates falling within her reach.

  3. A sign for humanity

    The infant’s testimony

    When Maryam returned to her people carrying the child, they accused her. She kept her silence and pointed to her son — and the infant ‘Isa spoke from the cradle, declaring: “Indeed I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet” (19:30). By his first words, Allah cleared his mother’s name and made the truth of the matter plain.

Her rank

Chosen above the women of the worlds

يَـٰمَرْيَمُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱصْطَفَىٰكِ وَطَهَّرَكِ وَٱصْطَفَىٰكِ عَلَىٰ نِسَآءِ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

“O Maryam, indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you, and chosen you above the women of the worlds.”

Qur’an 3:42

Islam honours Maryam because Allah honoured her. She stands in the Qur’an as a model for every believer — man and woman — of chastity, patience, trust in Allah and devotion in worship. Our masjid carries her name for exactly this reason: as a daily reminder of taqwa and of complete reliance on Allah.

Many of our neighbours of other backgrounds also hold Mary in deep esteem, and are often surprised — and moved — to learn how the Qur’an speaks of her. If that is you, you are very welcome to read Surah Maryam with us and ask anything you like.
